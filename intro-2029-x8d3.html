The last year

This is the final round. You are still the management team of your shipping company, and only your net worth at the end of 2029 counts: cash + ship values − loans. You need to decide which ships to keep, sell or buy, how to pay for any new ones, and whether each ship works on spot or on charter.

Everything you need is in the app

There you have today’s news (Thursday 4 January), today’s market data, market expectations, your result from last year, charts to help you visualise the numbers, and a calculator (“What if…?”) where you can test all your options.

Using this data, you need to make the decisions below:

  • For each ship you own: spot (the market rate, revealed at the end), a 1-year charter (the rate on screen, fixed), lay-up, sell at today’s price, or scrap (old ships only).
  • Buy more ships: old, eco, or none.
  • How to pay for new ships: cash, or the 50% bank loan.

What to look at before you decide

  1. Supply vs demand this year: has the balance between fleet and cargo changed since last year?
  2. The FFA: compare this year’s forecast with last year’s spot. Up or down?
  3. Ship prices: compare today’s prices with two years ago. Your score counts ship values at the end of the year.
  4. Charter or spot: is the charter offer above or below the FFA?
  5. How much can you afford: with the loan a ship costs you half in cash, but the loan payments still come this year.
  6. Survive being wrong, one last time: a forced sale at year end hurts your final score. Check the left box in “What if…?”.
  7. No exit needed: the game ends after 2029. What counts is what you hold on the last day.

The optimist and the pessimist in the news may each be right about a different year. Check their claims against the numbers.

No need to calculate anything: your team app does all the maths. Try your options there, then send your decision from the app.

Rules to keep in mind

  • Loan: 50% of the price. Each year you repay 10% of the original loan plus 7% interest.
  • Cash below zero at year end: the bank sells your ships at the year-end price.
  • Old ships earn $4,000 a day less than eco on spot and $3,000 less on charter (fixed simplifying assumptions).
  • Lay-up: no income, 30% of the running costs.

Glossary

Kamsarmax
A dry bulk ship of about 82,000 tonnes deadweight that carries grain, coal and bauxite. It is the largest size that can load at Port Kamsar in Guinea and still pass through the Panama Canal.
Eco ship
A modern design that burns less fuel. In the game: built 2021, costs more, earns more per day.
Spot rate
What a ship earns per day on the open market. It changes every day; the game uses the yearly average.
1-year charter
A contract that hires out your ship for one year at a fixed daily rate, agreed before the year starts.
FFA (forward freight agreement)
A contract traded today on a future freight rate: the market’s forecast of spot. It can be wrong.
Fleet growth
How much the number of ships grows in a year, after old ships are scrapped.
Order book
Ships already ordered from shipyards and not yet delivered. It tells you next year’s fleet growth.
Cargo demand (tonne-miles)
How much cargo is carried, multiplied by how far it travels. Longer routes need more ships.
Lay-up
Parking a ship with no work: no income, lower running costs.
Scrap
Selling a ship for its steel. In the game: $6m, old ships only.
Running costs
Crew, insurance, repairs and supplies to keep a ship working, paid every day of the year.
Net worth
Cash + ship values − loans. Your score.

Three rules for good decisions

Good managers don’t need to guess right. They need to survive being wrong.

Don’t pick a forecaster. Check their claims against supply, demand and the FFA, size your bet so you survive if you’re wrong, and decide your exit in advance.
